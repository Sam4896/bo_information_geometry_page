Abstract

Abstract

We study Bayesian optimization (BO) through the lens of information geometry. Pulling back the Fisher information metric through the surrogate posterior map yields a local sensitivity tensor on the input space, which leads to an upper bound on the gradient of reparameterizable acquisition functions (AFs). This view explains vanishing-gradient behavior in high-dimensional BO and provides a common interpretation of heuristics such as RAASP and dimension-scaled lengthscales. The upper bound can also serve as a generic recipe to understand the behavior of new acquisition functions or surrogates in high input dimensions as well. Using the bounds, we also identify necessary conditions for an input location to be an optimizer of the AF for a univariate Gaussian predictive posterior. Building on this analysis, we propose FITR, a trust-region-based BO method that replaces lengthscale-based scaling with local pullback-Fisher weights. FITR is not restricted to GP kernels with explicit lengthscales and easily generalizes to non-isotropic surrogates. FITR shows competitive performance on several benchmarks.

Background and references

Papenmeier et al. (2025) analyze why RAASP helps acquisition optimization, while Hvarfner et al. (2024) show the importance of dimension-scaled lengthscale priors. Our manifold view gives a common theoretical explanation for both.

Mathematical explanation

The acquisition function is generally non-convex. A stationary point like saddle point, maxima or minima (local or global) have zero gradient . Using the manifold view we show novel properties for those stationary points that can be used by future optimization methods for better acquistion function optimization.

Using the induced geometry as a trust region

FITR uses the pullback tensor to enhance TuRBO.

Our manifold view extends TuRBO (Eriksson et al. (2019)) to differentiable surrogates without explicit lengthscales. FITR uses the local pullback tensor to shape the region instead of kernel lengthscales, while retaining TuRBO's size adaptation and restarts. We test it using GPs with SE and IBNN kernels.
